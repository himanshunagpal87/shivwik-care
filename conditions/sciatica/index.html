Physiotherapy for sciatica

Home › Sciatica Physiotherapy

Sciatica is pain that travels from the lower back or buttock down the leg, caused by irritation of the sciatic nerve. It often settles over some weeks. Physiotherapy may help with positions that ease the pain, exercises to keep you moving, and a gradual return to activity. Shivwik Holistic Care in Paschim Vihar begins with a nerve and movement assessment.

What does sciatica feel like?

  • pain, burning or an electric feeling down the back of the thigh, calf or foot
  • tingling or numbness in part of the leg or foot
  • pain that is worse with sitting, bending, coughing or sneezing
  • sometimes weakness in the foot or leg

Not all leg pain is sciatica. An assessment checks the nerve, the back and the hip to see where the pain is coming from. This page does not diagnose your pain.

How does physiotherapy help?

  • Finding positions and movements that ease the pain, and those to avoid for now
  • Gentle exercises chosen for your pattern of symptoms
  • Hands-on treatment for stiffness and muscle tension
  • Advice on sitting, sleeping, lifting and staying active
  • Gradual strengthening once the pain settles, to lower the chance of it returning

Should I rest or keep moving?

Short periods of rest are fine when the pain is strong, but staying as active as you comfortably can usually helps more than bed rest. Short walks and frequent changes of position are a good start.

When is sciatica an emergency?

Go to a hospital emergency department straight away if you have numbness around the genitals or anus, new difficulty passing or controlling urine or stools, or weakness or numbness in both legs. These can be signs of pressure on the nerves that needs urgent treatment.

See a doctor soon if the leg is becoming weaker, the pain is severe and not easing, or you also have fever or unexplained weight loss.

Questions patients ask

How long does sciatica last?

Many people improve over some weeks, but it differs from person to person and we cannot promise a time. If it is not improving, or is getting worse, see your doctor.

Do I need an MRI?

Not always. A scan is usually considered when symptoms are severe, there is weakness, or the pain does not improve. Your doctor decides. Bring any reports you already have.

Is exercise safe with sciatica?

The right exercises are a standard part of care. Which ones suit you depends on your assessment, so please do not copy a routine made for someone else.
