Physiotherapy for knee pain and after knee replacement

Home › Knee Pain and Physiotherapy After Knee Replacement

Physiotherapy for knee pain strengthens the muscles that support the knee, improves movement and helps you stay active. After knee replacement surgery it restores bending, straightening, strength and walking in stages, following your surgeon's advice. Physiotherapy for knee pain and osteoarthritis

Knee pain is common with osteoarthritis, after an injury, or from overuse. Exercise is a core part of care for knee osteoarthritis. A programme may include:

  • Strengthening of the thigh and hip muscles, which take load off the knee
  • Movement and flexibility work to reduce stiffness
  • Balance and walking practice
  • Advice on stairs, sitting on the floor, footwear and pacing activity
  • Pain-relief measures suited to your condition

Keeping weight in a healthy range and staying active both help knees. Your physiotherapist can suggest activities that are comfortable for you.

Physiotherapy after knee replacement

Rehabilitation usually begins in hospital and continues after discharge. The aims, in order, are:

  • controlling swelling and pain
  • regaining the ability to straighten and bend the knee
  • rebuilding thigh strength
  • walking safely with a walker or stick, then without
  • managing stairs and daily tasks

Please bring your discharge summary and any instructions from your surgeon. We work within them.

What should I watch for after surgery?

Contact your surgeon or go to a hospital if the knee or wound becomes hot, red and more swollen, if there is discharge or fever, if you have calf pain or swelling, or sudden breathlessness.

When should I see a doctor first for knee pain?

See a doctor if the knee is very swollen after an injury, locks or gives way, cannot take your weight, or is hot and red with fever.

Questions patients ask

Can physiotherapy help me avoid knee surgery?

Exercise and physiotherapy help many people with knee osteoarthritis manage pain and stay active. Whether surgery is needed is a decision for you and your orthopaedic doctor, and no one can promise that it will be avoided.

When does physiotherapy start after knee replacement?

Your surgeon decides. It commonly begins soon after the operation and continues after you go home.

Is it normal for the knee to be stiff after surgery?

Some stiffness and swelling are expected in the early weeks and are worked on in rehabilitation. Tell your physiotherapist and surgeon if bending is not improving.
