## What can physiotherapy help with after a stroke?

- weakness or stiffness of an arm and leg on one side
- difficulty sitting, standing or keeping balance
- walking, with or without a stick or walker
- everyday tasks such as getting out of bed, using the bathroom and climbing stairs
- shoulder pain and care of the weak arm
- breathing and general fitness after a hospital stay

Recovery after a stroke differs greatly from person to person. We set goals with you and your family, and review them regularly.

## What does a session involve?

- **Assessment** of movement, strength, balance, sensation and what the person can do now
- **Task practice**: repeating real movements such as standing up, stepping and reaching
- **Strength and balance training**, with support for safety
- **Walking practice** and advice on aids
- **Stretching and positioning** to manage stiffness
- **Training for the family**: safe ways to help with transfers, walking and exercises at home

## How can the family help at home?

Regular short practice matters more than occasional long sessions. We give a home programme and show a family member how to assist safely. Please bring the discharge summary, prescriptions and scan reports to the first visit.

## Know the warning signs of a new stroke

Call for emergency help and go to a hospital immediately if there is sudden drooping of the face, weakness or numbness of an arm or leg, slurred or confused speech, sudden loss of vision, severe headache or loss of balance. Do not wait to see whether it passes.

## Questions patients ask

Q: When should physiotherapy start after a stroke?
A: As soon as the treating doctor says the person is medically stable. It usually begins in hospital and continues after discharge.

Q: Will the affected arm or leg recover fully?
A: No one can promise that. Many people regain useful movement with regular rehabilitation, and progress can continue for a long time. We work towards the goals that matter most to the person.

Q: Can the patient come to the clinic, or is home treatment needed?
A: Please message or call us to discuss the person's condition and mobility, and we will advise what is suitable.
