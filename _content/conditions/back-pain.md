## What causes lower back pain?

Often there is no single cause. Long hours of sitting, sudden increases in activity, lifting awkwardly, weak or tired muscles, stress and poor sleep can all play a part. An assessment looks at which of these apply to you. This page does not diagnose your pain.

## How does physiotherapy help?

- **Hands-on treatment** to ease stiffness and muscle tightness
- **Exercises** to restore movement and build strength in the back, hips and trunk
- **Advice** on sitting, desk set-up, lifting and sleeping positions
- **A plan for flare-ups**, so that you know what to do when the pain returns

## Physiotherapist or chiropractor for back pain?

A physiotherapist assesses how you move, treats with hands-on techniques and exercise, and teaches you to manage the problem yourself. Chiropractic care centres on spinal manipulation. Whichever you choose, look for a proper assessment, a clear explanation and a plan that includes exercise, and be cautious of anyone who promises a cure or asks you to commit to a long course in advance.

## Should I rest in bed?

Prolonged bed rest is generally not advised for ordinary back pain. Gentle movement within comfort, short walks and a gradual return to normal activity usually help more.

## When should I see a doctor first?

See a doctor promptly if back pain follows a fall or accident, or if you have numbness around the groin or inner thighs, loss of bladder or bowel control, weakness in a leg, fever, unexplained weight loss, a history of cancer, or pain that is constant and worse at night.

## Questions patients ask

Q: Do I need an MRI for back pain?
A: Usually not at the start. Scans are needed when the assessment or your doctor suspects a specific problem. If you already have reports, please bring them.

Q: I sit for work all day. What can I do?
A: Change position often, stand or walk for a couple of minutes every half hour, and set up your chair and screen properly. Read [managing lower back pain from long hours of sitting](/articles/managing-lower-back-pain-from-long-hours-of-sitting/).

Q: Is it safe to exercise when my back hurts?
A: Gentle, guided exercise is a standard part of care for back pain. We choose exercises that suit your stage and comfort, and progress them gradually.
