## Who is this for?

- older adults who feel unsteady or have had a fall
- people who have become weaker or slower after an illness or hospital stay
- those with joint stiffness, arthritis or osteoporosis
- people living with Parkinson's disease, or recovering from a stroke
- anyone who wants to stay active and independent for longer

## Why do falls matter?

Falls are a leading cause of injury in older people, and fear of falling often makes people move less, which in turn makes them weaker and less steady. Balance and strength can be trained at any age.

## What does the programme include?

- **Assessment** of balance, walking, strength and the medicines and conditions that affect steadiness
- **Balance training**, progressed safely with support nearby
- **Leg and core strengthening**, especially for getting up from a chair and climbing stairs
- **Walking practice**, with or without a stick or walker
- **Home safety advice**: lighting, footwear, bathroom and bedside arrangements
- **A simple home routine** that a family member can help with

## Is exercise safe at my age?

For most older adults, the right kind of exercise is safer than inactivity. We start gently, keep you supported, and take your heart, lung and bone health into account. Please bring your prescriptions and reports.

## When should I see a doctor first?

See a doctor if falls are happening with blackouts, dizziness on standing, chest pain, sudden weakness on one side, or a sudden change in walking or memory. After any fall with severe pain or inability to stand, go to a hospital.

## Questions patients ask

Q: My parent is afraid of falling and avoids walking. Can you help?
A: Yes. Fear of falling is common and can be addressed with graded balance work and practice in a safe setting. A family member is welcome to attend.

Q: Can someone with osteoporosis exercise?
A: Usually yes, with the right choice of exercises. Read [how to exercise safely with osteoporosis](/articles/exercise-safely-with-osteoporosis-protect-your-bones-and-stay-active/) and get an assessment before starting.

Q: How often should an older person exercise?
A: Little and often works best. We give a short routine that fits into the day and adjust it as strength and balance improve.
