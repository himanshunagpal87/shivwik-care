## What is manual therapy used for?

It may help with:

- neck pain and stiffness, including pain from long hours at a screen
- shoulder pain and restricted movement, such as frozen shoulder
- lower back pain and stiffness
- tightness after an injury or a period of immobilisation

Related pages: [neck pain](/conditions/neck-pain/), [back pain](/conditions/back-pain/) and [frozen shoulder](/conditions/frozen-shoulder/).

## What does a session involve?

After an assessment of your posture, movement and the painful area, your physiotherapist may use:

- **Joint mobilisation**: gentle, graded movements of a stiff joint
- **Myofascial release and soft-tissue work**: sustained pressure and stretching of tight muscles
- **Assisted stretching** and movement re-education

Hands-on treatment is usually followed by exercises to do at home. Manual therapy on its own gives short-term ease for many people. The exercises are what help you keep it.

## Is manual therapy painful?

Techniques are applied within your comfort. You may feel pressure or a stretch, and some people feel mild soreness for a day afterwards. Tell your physiotherapist at once if anything feels sharp or wrong.

## When is manual therapy not suitable?

Some conditions need a medical opinion first, for example a recent fracture, osteoporosis, an infection, cancer, or new numbness or weakness. Please mention your full medical history and bring your reports to the assessment.

## Questions patients ask

Q: Is manual therapy the same as massage or chiropractic treatment?
A: No. Manual therapy is part of physiotherapy. It follows a clinical assessment and is combined with exercise and advice. It is not a general massage, and it is different from chiropractic care.

Q: Will one session fix my pain?
A: Some people feel easier after one session, but lasting change usually needs a plan that includes exercise and changes to daily habits. Your physiotherapist will explain what to expect after assessing you.

Q: Do I need an X-ray or MRI before coming?
A: Not always. If you already have reports, please bring them. If your assessment suggests that a scan or a doctor's opinion is needed, we will tell you.
