## Which injuries do you see?

- ankle sprains and knee ligament injuries
- muscle strains in the hamstring, calf or groin
- shoulder and elbow overuse problems
- runner's knee, shin pain and heel pain
- rehabilitation after sports surgery, as advised by your surgeon

## What are the stages of rehabilitation?

- **Stage 1: Settle the injury**: protect the area, manage pain and swelling, and keep the rest of the body moving.
- **Stage 2: Restore movement**: regain normal range in the joint.
- **Stage 3: Rebuild strength and control**: progressive loading, balance and stability work.
- **Stage 4: Return to sport**: drills that match your sport, then a graded return to training.

How quickly you move through the stages depends on the injury and how you respond. We do not set a fixed date in advance.

## Should I rest completely?

Usually not for long. Most injuries do better with the right kind of early movement than with complete rest. What is right for you depends on the injury, so get it assessed before you train through pain.

## Clinic or home exercises?

Both have a place. The clinic is where the injury is assessed, hands-on treatment is given and exercises are taught and progressed. Home is where most of the repetitions happen. We give you a clear home programme and review it at each visit.

## When should I see a doctor first?

Go to a doctor or hospital if you cannot put weight on the limb, the joint looks deformed, there is severe swelling soon after the injury, or you have numbness or a cold, pale limb. A fracture or serious ligament injury needs medical care first.

## Questions patients ask

Q: When can I return to my sport?
A: It depends on the injury and on how your strength and control recover. We test these as you progress and tell you when each step is reasonable.

Q: Does kinesio taping help?
A: Taping may give support or awareness of a joint for some people, but it is an addition to rehabilitation, not a replacement. Read [what kinesio taping can and cannot do](/articles/kinesio-taping-what-it-can-and-cannot-do/).

Q: I am not an athlete. Can I still come?
A: Yes. The same approach applies to gym, running, yoga and weekend sport injuries.
