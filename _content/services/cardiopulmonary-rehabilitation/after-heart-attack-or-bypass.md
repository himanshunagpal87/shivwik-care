## Why is rehabilitation advised after a heart event?

After a heart attack or heart surgery, many people feel unsure how much they can safely do. Some avoid activity altogether, and others do too much too soon. Cardiac rehabilitation gives you a supervised, step-by-step way to become active again, alongside the medicines and follow-up your cardiologist has prescribed.

## When can I start?

Your cardiologist or surgeon decides when you are ready. Please bring your discharge summary, prescription and recent reports, such as an ECG or echocardiogram, to your first visit. We plan within any limits your doctor has set.

## What does the programme include?

- **Assessment** of your current activity, breathing, pulse and blood pressure response
- **Graded walking and exercise**, increased gradually and monitored during the session
- **Breathing and chest care**, which is especially useful after bypass surgery
- **Care of the chest wound and posture** after surgery, including safe ways to get up, cough and lift
- **Guidance for home**: how to pace activity, climb stairs and recognise when to stop

## What should I watch for at home?

Stop the activity and seek medical help if you have chest pain or pressure, unusual breathlessness, dizziness, fainting or a fast or irregular heartbeat. Do not wait for your next session.

## Questions patients ask

Q: How soon after bypass surgery can I begin physiotherapy at a clinic?
A: That depends on your recovery and your surgeon's advice. Bring your discharge summary and we will plan from there.

Q: Will I be monitored while exercising?
A: Yes. Your pulse, breathing and how you feel are checked during each session, and the exercise is adjusted to match.

Q: Can rehabilitation replace my heart medicines?
A: No. Rehabilitation works alongside the treatment your cardiologist has prescribed. Please do not stop or change any medicine without your doctor's advice.
