Your first visit

What happens in the first visit?

The first visit is an assessment. Your physiotherapist asks about your symptoms, daily routine and medical history, then checks posture, movement, strength and the area that troubles you. You then get a plan made for your condition. Our article on your first physiotherapy visit explains each step.

What should I bring?

Bring any recent medical files related to your condition: prescriptions, X-rays, MRI or other reports, and discharge papers if you have had surgery or a hospital stay. It also helps to note when the problem started and what makes it better or worse.

What should I wear?

Wear loose, comfortable clothes, such as track pants and a T-shirt, so that the area being assessed can move freely.

Treatment

Which conditions do you treat?

We treat heart and lung conditions through cardiopulmonary rehabilitation, back and neck pain, sciatica, joint and sports injuries, recovery after surgery or fracture, balance and mobility problems in later life, and pregnancy-related aches. See all services.

How many sessions will I need?

It depends on your condition, how long you have had it and how your body responds. After the assessment your physiotherapist explains the plan, what you can do at home and how progress will be reviewed.

What is cardiopulmonary rehabilitation?

It is a supervised programme of breathing exercises, graded physical activity and practical guidance for people with heart or lung conditions, for example after a heart attack or bypass surgery, or with COPD, asthma or breathlessness after COVID. It is planned around your doctor's advice.

Safety

When should I see a doctor first instead of a physiotherapist?

Please see a doctor or go to a hospital emergency department if you have chest pain, sudden breathlessness, sudden severe pain after a fall or accident, new weakness or numbness, loss of bladder or bowel control, or fever and unexplained weight loss along with pain.
