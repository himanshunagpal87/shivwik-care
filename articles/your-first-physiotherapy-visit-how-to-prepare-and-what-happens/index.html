Your First Physiotherapy Visit: How to Prepare and What Happens

Learn what happens during an initial physiotherapy assessment and how you can prepare for your first appointment.

Shivwik Holistic Care team · October 2026 · 2 min read
Home › Articles › Your First Physiotherapy Visit: How to Prepare and What Happens

Starting your physiotherapy journey can feel unfamiliar if you have never visited a physio before. Whether you are dealing with nagging back stiffness, recovering from a joint injury, or managing long-term discomfort, knowing what happens during your first session can help you feel relaxed and confident.

Understanding the Initial Assessment

Your first appointment is primarily about getting to know your health background and evaluating how your body moves. Your physiotherapist will start by asking simple questions about your current discomfort, daily routine, medical history, and personal health goals.

After discussing your history, a thorough physical assessment takes place. This typically includes:

  • Checking your posture, balance, and how you walk.
  • Testing joint mobility and flexibility in the affected area.
  • Assessing muscle strength and tenderness.
  • Observing movements that trigger discomfort or feel restricted.

This evaluation helps your therapist understand the underlying cause of your movement difficulty and design a personalized plan tailored to your daily routine.

How to Prepare for Your Visit

A little preparation ensures you get the most out of your evaluation. Here are a few practical steps to prepare for your appointment:

  • Wear comfortable clothing: Choose loose-fitting clothes, such as track pants and a comfortable t-shirt, so your therapist can easily examine your movement.
  • Bring medical records: Carry any recent medical files, X-rays, MRI reports, or doctor prescriptions related to your condition.
  • Note down your symptoms: Think about when the discomfort started, what activities make it better or worse, and any specific questions you want to ask.
  • Arrive slightly early: Giving yourself a few extra minutes allows you to complete any basic intake forms without feeling rushed.

Gentle Steps to Keep Moving

While waiting for your assessment, gentle movement can often help manage day-to-day stiffness. Simple activities like light indoor walking or gentle shoulder rolls can keep your body active without strain.

If you try basic mobility exercises at home, keep them light and pain-free. Always get assessed by a professional first, and stop immediately if any movement increases your pain.

When to See a Doctor Promptly

While physical therapy is helpful for many joint and muscle issues, certain warning signs require urgent attention from a medical doctor. Seek prompt medical care if you experience:

  • Sudden, severe pain following a major fall or accident.
  • Numbness, weakness, or a pins-and-needles sensation spreading down both legs or arms.
  • Unexpected changes in bowel or bladder control.
  • Unexplained fever, chills, or sudden weight loss alongside pain.

If you notice any of these red flags, please consult a physician or visit a hospital emergency room right away.
