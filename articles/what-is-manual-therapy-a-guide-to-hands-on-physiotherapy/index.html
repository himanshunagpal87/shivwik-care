What Is Manual Therapy? A Guide to Hands-On Physiotherapy

Learn how manual therapy uses hands-on techniques to ease stiffness and support recovery, and what to expect during your physiotherapy session.

Shivwik Holistic Care team · October 2026 · 3 min read
Home › Articles › What Is Manual Therapy? A Guide to Hands-On Physiotherapy

When you hear about physiotherapy, you might think of exercises, stretches, or specialized equipment. However, one of the most effective and reassuring parts of physical rehabilitation is manual therapy. This is a hands-on approach where a trained physiotherapist uses their hands to assess, treat, and mobilize your muscles, joints, and soft tissues.

If you are dealing with lingering back tightness, neck discomfort, or stiff joints, understanding how hands-on care works can help you feel completely comfortable before your first visit.

What Is Manual Therapy?

Manual therapy refers to a group of specialized techniques performed directly by a physiotherapist's hands rather than machines. Instead of relying solely on exercise equipment, your therapist uses precise pressure and skilled movements to treat specific areas of discomfort.

Common techniques include:

  • Soft tissue mobilization: Gentle pressure and rhythmic strokes applied to muscles and tendons to relieve tightness and improve circulation.
  • Joint mobilization: Slow, measured movements applied to stiff joints to help restore their natural range of motion.
  • Passive stretching: Guided stretches where the therapist gently moves your limb to help relax tight tissues.

These techniques aim to reduce muscle tension, ease joint pressure, and help your body move with greater freedom.

What to Expect During a Session

Your first session always begins with a detailed conversation and physical assessment. Your therapist will examine your posture, check how your joints move, and identify areas of tenderness or muscle tightness.

During the manual therapy treatment, you will usually lie or sit comfortably. Your therapist will explain each movement before applying gentle, controlled pressure. You might feel mild pressure or a deep stretch, but the process should generally feel manageable and relieving. Communication is key, so you can always let your therapist know if a technique feels too intense.

Simple Exercises to Support Your Movement

While hands-on care helps ease tightness, gentle movement at home keeps your joints moving smoothly. Here are two simple exercises that are often recommended:

  • Shoulder Rolls: Stand or sit comfortably. Gently roll your shoulders upward, backward, and down in a smooth circular motion. Repeat 5 to 10 times to ease upper back tightness.
  • Gentle Knee-to-Chest Stretch: Lie comfortably on your back on a firm bed or mat. Slowly pull one knee up toward your chest holding it gently with both hands for a few seconds, then lower it back down.

Always consult a qualified physiotherapist to get assessed before starting any new movement routine. If you feel any sharp pain or increased discomfort while performing these movements, stop immediately.

Red Flags: When to Consult a Doctor

Manual therapy is safe for most people, but certain severe symptoms require immediate medical attention. Please consult a doctor promptly if you experience:

  • Sudden, unmanageable pain following an injury or fall
  • Numbness, tingling, or weakness spreading down your arms or legs
  • Unexplained high fever accompanied by joint swelling or severe pain
  • Sudden loss of bowel or bladder control
