Understanding Frozen Shoulder: Stages and Physiotherapy Support

Learn what causes frozen shoulder, how its three stages progress, and how gentle physiotherapy can help restore your shoulder mobility safely.

Shivwik Holistic Care team · November 2026 · 3 min read
Home › Articles › Understanding Frozen Shoulder: Stages and Physiotherapy Support

Shoulder pain that gradually turns into severe stiffness can make everyday tasks difficult. Simple actions like reaching for a shelf, combing your hair, or dressing up can feel challenging. This condition is often known as frozen shoulder (adhesive capsulitis), a common issue where the capsule surrounding the shoulder joint becomes thick, tight, and inflamed.

The Three Stages of Frozen Shoulder

Frozen shoulder usually develops slowly and progresses through three distinct phases:

  • The Freezing Stage: In this initial phase, any movement of your shoulder causes pain, and your range of motion slowly begins to decrease. Pain may feel worse at night.
  • The Frozen Stage: The pain might gradually lessen during this period, but the stiffness worsens. Moving your arm in any direction becomes very difficult.
  • The Thawing Stage: Mobility in your shoulder slowly starts to improve as the joint capsule gradually relaxes.

How Physiotherapy Supports Recovery

Physiotherapy plays an important role throughout each stage of a frozen shoulder. Rather than pushing through severe pain, guided care focuses on gentle, progressive movements tailored to how your joint feels.

During the early stage, therapy focuses on managing discomfort and protecting your remaining movement. As stiffness becomes the main concern, structured stretching and mobility work help maintain muscle strength around the joint and encourage gradual movement. A tailored program helps ensure you move safely without causing further irritation to the joint capsule.

Gentle Exercises to Support Shoulder Mobility

Before starting any exercise, it is essential to be assessed by a healthcare professional to ensure these movements are appropriate for your specific condition. If any movement increases your pain, stop immediately.

  • Pendulum Swings: Lean forward slightly while supporting yourself with your healthy arm on a sturdy table. Let your affected arm hang down freely. Gently sway your arm in small circles, using momentum rather than muscle force.
  • Towel Stretch: Hold a small towel behind your back with both hands. Gently pull upward with your top hand so that the lower arm receives a mild, comfortable stretch. Hold briefly and release.
  • Wall Crawl: Stand facing a wall at arm's length. Slowly walk your fingers up the wall as far as comfortable without straining, then slowly walk them back down.

When to See a Doctor Promptly

While frozen shoulder is common, certain symptoms require prompt medical evaluation by a physician. Seek immediate medical attention if you experience:

  • Pain accompanied by high fever, redness, or warmth around the joint.
  • Shoulder pain following a sudden injury, fall, or severe trauma.
  • Unexplained weight loss or feeling generally unwell alongside shoulder stiffness.
  • Sudden loss of feeling, numbness, or tingling traveling down your arm or hand.
