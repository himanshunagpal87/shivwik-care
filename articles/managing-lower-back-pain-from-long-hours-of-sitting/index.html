Managing Lower Back Pain from Long Hours of Sitting

Learn how long hours of sitting cause lower back pain, simple movement habits to ease discomfort, and when physiotherapy can help.

Shivwik Holistic Care team · October 2026 · 3 min read
Home › Articles › Managing Lower Back Pain from Long Hours of Sitting

Whether you work at a desk for long hours, spend time in traffic, or sit while relaxing at home, sitting for extended periods is a major cause of lower back pain today. When we sit for too long, the muscles supporting our spine become tired, tight, and weak. Over time, this causes stiffness, dull aches, and discomfort that can disrupt your daily routine.

Why Long Sitting Strains Your Lower Back

Our spines are built to move. Sitting naturally flattens the normal curve of your lower back, placing higher pressure on the spinal discs and surrounding muscles than standing does.

When you remain seated for hours without breaks:

  • Tight hips: The muscles at the front of your hips shorten and pull on your lower back.
  • Weak core: Your abdominal and gluteal muscles stay relaxed, leaving your back to carry extra strain.
  • Disc compression: Constant pressure can lead to persistent tightness or deep muscular aches.

Posture and Daily Movement Breaks

Good posture does not mean sitting rigid and frozen. It means changing your position often and supporting your spine properly.

  • Adjust your seat: Keep your feet flat on the ground with your knees slightly lower than or level with your hips.
  • Support your back: Use a small cushion or lumbar roll behind your lower back to maintain its natural curve.
  • Take regular breaks: Stand up, stretch, or walk around for two minutes every 30 to 45 minutes. Setting a quick timer can help remind you to move.

Gentle Exercises to Try at Home

Before starting any new exercise routine, it is best to be evaluated by a healthcare professional. If any movement causes sharp pain or increases your discomfort, stop right away.

  • Gentle Standing Extension: Stand comfortably with your hands on your lower back. Gently lean backwards slightly, keeping your knees straight, and return to standing. Repeat slowly 3 to 5 times.
  • Seated Cat-Cow: Sit near the edge of your chair with feet flat. Inhale as you gently arch your back and look slightly upward. Exhale as you round your spine, bringing your chin gently toward your chest. Repeat slowly for 5 breaths.
  • Knee-to-Chest Stretch: Lie on your back on a firm bed or mat. Slowly pull one knee toward your chest using both hands, hold for 15 seconds, and lower it back down. Repeat on the other side.

Warning Signs That Need Prompt Medical Care

Most lower back stiffness improves with movement and rest. However, certain symptoms require immediate evaluation by a qualified doctor:

  • Numbness, tingling, or weakness in your legs or feet.
  • Pain that travels down past your knees.
  • Sudden difficulty controlling your bladder or bowels.
  • Back pain accompanied by unexplained fever or sudden weight loss.

How Physiotherapy Can Help

Physiotherapy focuses on finding the root cause of your back discomfort. A personalized rehabilitation program helps strengthen weak muscles, improve joint flexibility, and teach safe movement habits suited to your lifestyle.
