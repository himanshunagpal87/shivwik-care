Set Up Your Home Desk for Pain-Free Work

Learn how to create a comfortable workspace and routine to prevent pain while working from home.

Shivwik Holistic Care team · October 2026 · 2 min read
Home › Articles › Set Up Your Home Desk for Pain-Free Work

Working from home has become more common in recent years. However, setting up your workspace correctly can help you avoid unnecessary aches and pains. Follow these simple steps to ensure your home office setup is both comfortable and healthy.

Choose the Right Space

  • Opt for a well-lit area to reduce eye strain.
  • Ensure the space is quiet to minimize distractions.
  • Choose a location near a window for natural light, which can boost your mood and energy levels.

Ergonomic Desk Setup

  • Invest in a comfortable chair that supports your lower back. Adjust the height so your feet are flat on the floor.
  • Position your computer monitor at eye level to avoid neck strain. Keep it about an arm’s length away.
  • Use a document holder to keep reading materials close to the monitor.
  • Place a small footrest if your feet don’t touch the floor.

Proper Posture

  • Sit upright with your back straight and shoulders relaxed.
  • Keep your knees at a 90-degree angle and your feet flat on the floor.
  • Position your computer screen to avoid glare from lights, reducing eye strain.
  • Take regular breaks to stretch and move around.

Daily Routine for Health

  • Start your day with a gentle warm-up. Try shoulder rolls, neck stretches, and arm circles.
  • Stay hydrated by drinking plenty of water throughout the day.
  • Incorporate short walking breaks to stretch your legs and improve circulation.
  • End your day with a few minutes of deep breathing or a simple meditation to relieve stress.

Warning Signs

  • If you experience persistent pain or discomfort, consult a healthcare professional promptly.
  • Seek medical advice if you notice any changes in your vision or experience headaches.
  • Pay attention to any numbness or tingling in your hands or arms, which could indicate nerve compression.

Gentle Exercises to Try

  • Shoulder Rolls: Roll your shoulders forward and backward in a circular motion.
  • Neck Stretch: Gently tilt your head to one side, bringing your ear toward your shoulder, and hold for 15 seconds. Repeat on the other side.
  • Arm Circles: Hold your arms outstretched, make small circles with your hands, then gradually increase the size. Reverse direction halfway through.

Remember, it’s important to get assessed by a physiotherapist to ensure your exercises are appropriate and safe for your individual needs.
