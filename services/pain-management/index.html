Physiotherapy for back pain, neck pain and other long-term pain

Home › Services › Physiotherapy for Long-Term Pain

Physiotherapy for long-term pain starts with finding out what is driving your pain, then combines hands-on treatment, exercise and changes to daily habits to help you move with more comfort. Shivwik Holistic Care in Paschim Vihar treats persistent back and neck pain, sciatica and other muscle and joint pain.

What kinds of pain do you treat?

  • lower back pain, including pain from long hours of sitting
  • neck and shoulder pain
  • sciatica and other pain that travels down a leg or arm
  • joint pain from osteoarthritis
  • pain that has continued after an injury has healed

We have separate pages on back pain, neck pain, sciatica and knee pain.

How does the assessment work?

Your physiotherapist asks how the pain started, what eases or worsens it, how you sleep and work, and what you have already tried. Posture, movement, strength and nerve function are then checked. The aim is to understand your pain, not only to locate it.

What does treatment include?

  • Hands-on treatment to ease stiffness and muscle tension
  • Exercise to restore movement and build strength gradually
  • Advice on daily habits: sitting, lifting, sleep and activity levels
  • Pacing, so that good days and bad days even out
  • Electrotherapy or taping where it suits your condition

Will the pain go away completely?

No honest clinic can promise that. Many people find their pain becomes less frequent and less limiting, and that they can do more. We set goals with you that are about what you want to be able to do.

When should I see a doctor first?

See a doctor promptly if pain follows a fall or accident, if you have numbness around the groin, loss of bladder or bowel control, weakness in a leg or arm, fever, unexplained weight loss, or pain that is constant and worse at night.

Questions patients ask

I have had back pain for years. Can physiotherapy still help?

It is worth an assessment. Long-standing pain often responds to a gradual programme of movement and strengthening, though results differ from person to person.

Should I rest when the pain flares up?

Short rest is fine, but staying in bed for days usually slows recovery. Gentle movement within comfort is generally better. Your physiotherapist will guide you on what to do during a flare-up.

Do I have to stop my painkillers?

No. Follow your doctor's advice about medicines. Physiotherapy works alongside them.
