Cardiopulmonary rehabilitation in Paschim Vihar, Delhi

Home › Services › Cardiopulmonary Rehabilitation in Paschim Vihar, Delhi

Cardiopulmonary rehabilitation is a supervised programme of breathing exercises, graded physical activity and day-to-day guidance for people with heart or lung conditions. At Shivwik Holistic Care in Paschim Vihar it is led by Dr. Sonal Khera Nagpal, whose master's degree (MPT) is in cardiopulmonary physiotherapy, and it is planned around your doctor's advice.

Who is cardiopulmonary rehabilitation for?

It may help if you are:

  • recovering after a heart attack, angioplasty or bypass surgery
  • living with COPD, asthma or another long-term lung condition
  • still breathless or easily tired after COVID or a chest infection
  • regaining strength after an ICU or long hospital stay

We have a page for each of the three most common reasons people come to us:

  • Cardiac rehabilitation after a heart attack or bypass
  • Pulmonary rehabilitation for COPD and asthma
  • Post-COVID physiotherapy for breathlessness

What happens in a session?

Every programme starts with an assessment of your breathing, your current activity level and your medical reports. A typical session may include:

  • Breathing exercises to help you breathe more comfortably and control breathlessness
  • Airway-clearance techniques where phlegm is a problem
  • Graded exercise, such as walking or gentle strengthening, increased step by step
  • Guidance for daily life: pacing, climbing stairs, sleeping positions and returning to activity

Your pulse, breathing and how you feel are checked during the session, and the plan is adjusted to match.

How is it planned with my doctor's advice?

Rehabilitation supports the treatment your cardiologist, chest physician or surgeon has given you. It does not replace it. Please bring your discharge summary, prescriptions and recent test reports so that the programme respects any limits your doctor has set.

How do I choose a physiotherapist for cardiac or pulmonary rehabilitation?

These are fair questions to ask any clinic:

  • Is the physiotherapist trained in cardiopulmonary physiotherapy?
  • Do they have hospital experience with heart and lung patients?
  • Will they read your reports and work within your doctor's advice?
  • Do they monitor you during exercise?

Dr. Sonal's MPT is in cardiopulmonary physiotherapy and her clinical background includes ICU critical care at St. Stephen's Hospital. You can read more about Dr. Sonal.

When should I see a doctor first?

Please contact your doctor or go to a hospital emergency department if you have chest pain or pressure, breathlessness at rest that is new or worsening, fainting, or a fast or irregular heartbeat. Rehabilitation starts only when your doctor considers you stable.

Questions patients ask

Do I need a referral for cardiopulmonary rehabilitation?

Please bring your doctor's discharge summary or prescription and recent reports. If you have had a recent heart attack, heart surgery or hospital admission, we plan the programme around what your doctor has advised.

Is exercise safe after a heart attack or with a lung condition?

Supervised, graded exercise is a standard part of heart and lung rehabilitation. What is safe for you depends on your condition and your doctor's advice, which is why we assess you first and monitor you during sessions.

How many sessions will I need?

It varies from person to person. After your assessment we explain the plan, what to do at home and how we will review your progress.
